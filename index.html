01 / THE STORY

Film Overview

In 1890 London, Sherlock Holmes and his partner Dr. Watson stop Lord Henry Blackwood, a nobleman accused of performing ritualistic murders. Blackwood warns Holmes that three more deaths will follow than is hanged. When Blackwood's tomb is broken into and a missing man's body is discovered inside, Holmes and Watson investigate and uncover science experiments. They also learn that Blackwood is connected to a secret society and plans to take control of the British Empire. Holmes discovers that Blackwood's supposed supernatural powers are actually tricks made possible through science. With help from Watson and Irene Adler, Holmes stops Blackwood's plan to poison Parliament. Blackwood is eventually killed during a fight with Holmes on the unfinished Tower Bridge. However, Adler reveals that her employer is Professor Moriarty, a dangerous criminal mastermind. Holmes then discovers that Moriarty used the events as a distraction to steal an important piece of technology, setting up their next confrontation.

02 / WHAT WE LEARNED

Connections to Our Course

01

Infrastructure

What it means: The basic physical systems and structures a society needs to function, such as roads, bridges, railroads, communication systems, and utilities.

Connection to the film:

In class, we talked about how new infrastructure came about to suit the developing industrial society of the 17th century. Sherlock Holmes (2009)'s setting shows this in great detail, with vast road systems, railroads, steamboats, canals, harbors, and sewers all being present throughout the movie.

Class reference: Chapter 4 / SLIDE #5

02

Social Darwinism

What it means: The belief that stronger or more successful people rise above weaker ones, applying Darwin’s theory of natural selection to human society. Used to justify wealth inequality and racism.

Connection to the film: In class, we discussed how during the 17th century, rich and powerful people used social Darwinism to justify their wealth that had been built on the backs of the poor lower class that lived in difficult conditions. Several scenes in Sherlock Holmes 2009 demonstrate this divide, such as The dinner scene, which depicts many wealthy, powerful people eating dinner at a very nice restaurant, and the scene directly after the jail scene, which depicts many poor workers cleaning dishes underneath someone's home in poor conditions with dirty clothes. aswell as many working on a bridge high in the air with no safety precautions.

Class reference: Chapter 6 / SLIDE #6

03

Mercantalism

What it means: An economic system and type of nationalist economic policy that is designed to maximize exports and minimize imports of an economy through exploitation of colonies for natural resources

Connection to the film: Throughout the movie, the main antagonist, Lord Blackwood, explains that he intends to take over the British Empire and retake the British colonies of the United States In order to use them to gain power. Directly aligns with the mercantilist beliefs of empires that we learned in the humanities textbook.

04 / CRITICAL RECEPTION

Entertainment Value & Reviews

What Critics Say

Critics were relatively positive, praising Robert Downey Jr.'s Sherlock, the visuals, and action. However, many noted the difference from traditional Sherlock Holmes stories in that this one is more action-packed and humorous rather than focusing on the mysterious and investigative aspects of the story.

My Evaluation

I quite enjoyed the movie. Robert Downey Jr. was excellent. There were many funny scenes. The sets looked very convincing, and the action was pretty good. While it wasn't a main focus, Sherlock's investigative skills were very entertaining and the mysteries had satisfying answers. I would recommend to anyone who enjoys action, comedy, and mysteries.

My thoughts did differ slightly from the critics in that I believed that the mysteries and detective skills were shown off more than enough, and the action added to the movie, while not completely outshining these more traditional Sherlock Holmes aspects.
