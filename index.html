01 / THE STORY

Film Overview

In 1890 London, Sherlock Holmes and his partner Dr. Watson stop Lord Henry Blackwood, a nobleman accused of performing ritualistic murders. Blackwood warns Holmes that three more deaths will follow than is hanged. When Blackwood's tomb is broken into and a missing man's body is discovered inside, Holmes and Watson investigate and uncover science experiments. They also learn that Blackwood is connected to a secret society and plans to take control of the British Empire. Holmes discovers that Blackwood's supposed supernatural powers are actually tricks made possible through science. With help from Watson and Irene Adler, Holmes stops Blackwood's plan to poison Parliament. Blackwood is eventually killed during a fight with Holmes on the unfinished Tower Bridge. However, Adler reveals that her employer is Professor Moriarty, a dangerous criminal mastermind. Holmes then discovers that Moriarty used the events as a distraction to steal an important piece of technology, setting up their next confrontation.

Infrastructure

What it means: The basic physical systems and structures a society needs to function, such as roads, bridges, railroads, communication systems, and utilities.

Connection to the film:

In class, we talked about how new infrastructure came about to suit the developing industrial society of the 19th century. Sherlock Holmes (2009)'s setting shows this in great detail, with vast road systems, railroads, steamboats, canals, harbors, and sewers all being present throughout the movie.

Social Darwinism

What it means: The belief that stronger or more successful people rise above weaker ones, applying Darwin’s theory of natural selection to human society. Used to justify wealth inequality and racism.

Connection to the film: In class, we discussed how during the 19th century, rich and powerful people used social Darwinism to justify their wealth that had been built on the backs of the poor lower class that lived in difficult conditions. Several scenes in Sherlock Holmes 2009 demonstrate this divide, such as The dinner scene, which depicts many wealthy, powerful people eating dinner at a very nice restaurant, and the scene directly after the jail scene, which depicts many poor workers cleaning dishes underneath someone's home in poor conditions with dirty clothes. aswell as many working on a bridge high in the air with no safety precautions.

03 / INNOVATION & IMPACT

Technology in the Film

Train

Thanks to the invention steam and diesel engines, railroads were able to connect newly industrialized societies of the Western world, starting in the late 1700s. The first public railroad opened in England and railroads quickly expanded in the United States. The first American railroads began operating in the 1830s, including the Baltimore and Ohio Railroad in 1830. Railroads expanded rapidly with help from private investors and some federal government support. They improved transportation, encouraged westward expansion, and helped connect different parts of the country. In Sherlock Holmes 2009, there is a scene where Irene gets onto a passenger car of a train To escape the dangerous situation of the mystery. However, she decides instead to stay and help Sherlock solve the mystery. In the end, taking a part of Blackwood's machine for the mysterious man she is working for.

Trains use steam, diesel, or electricity to move. A locomotive provides the power to pull railroad cars by burning fuel to create kinetic energy and spin the wheels, which can carry passengers, coal, chemicals, and other supplies. Railroad tracks guide a train along a low friction surface. All railroad cars have an undercarriage, then have several different cargoss on top. There are passenger cars, boxcars, ore cars, tank cars, trailer cars, and more.

Research sources: Library Of Congress, HowStuffWorks

SteamBoat

In Humanities class we learned that John Fitch created the first steamboat, and Robert Fulton made the first commercially successful one. Steamboats became an important form of transportation in the United States during the 1800s. They used steam-powered engines and paddle wheels to transport passengers, supplies, and goods along rivers and waterways. However, steamboats had disadvantages, including boiler explosions, fires, and environmental damage

Steamboats work by using heat from fuel to turn water into steam, turning the paddle to move the boat. In Sherlock Holmes 2009, there are several scenes with steamboats. One scene where one is seen in the harbor, and others where Sherlock Holmes and Watson use a boat to traverse London.

Research source: Wikipedia

Overall Technology Analysis

Sherlock Holmes, 2009, gives a great insight into infrastructure and transportation technologies of the late 19th century, including trains and steamboats. These technologies are presented with great accuracy for the time period, and despite not being a focal point of the movie, can help us gain some insights into life during the time period and how technological innovations changed society throughout history. Desppite how the story takes place in London, it can help us grasp the effects of these technologies that spread from Europe to the United States. These transportation technologies allowed for an interconnected United States and increased trade, westward expansion, and agriculture and business.

04 / CRITICAL RECEPTION

Entertainment Value & Reviews

What Critics Say

Critics were relatively positive, praising Robert Downey Jr.'s Sherlock, the visuals, and action. However, many noted the difference from traditional Sherlock Holmes stories in that this one is more action-packed and humorous rather than focusing on the mysterious and investigative aspects of the story.

General audiences were even more positive about the movie, likely looking over the unique, action-packed portrayal of the classic Sherlock Holmes story. While critics and more experienced Sherlock Holmes readers may have more trouble looking beyond this less traditional depiction.

My Evaluation

I quite enjoyed the movie. Robert Downey Jr. was excellent. There were many funny scenes. The sets looked very convincing, and the action was pretty good. While it wasn't a main focus, Sherlock's investigative skills were very entertaining and the mysteries had satisfying answers. I would recommend to anyone who enjoys action, comedy, and mysteries.

My thoughts did differ slightly from the critics in that I believed that the mysteries and detective skills were shown off more than enough, and the action added to the movie, while not completely outshining these more traditional Sherlock Holmes aspects.
